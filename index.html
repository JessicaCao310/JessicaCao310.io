A History of Chocolate

From sacred Mesoamerican drink to the bars we love today — four thousand years of one remarkable bean.

Four thousand years, one bean

Follow cacao's journey from sacred Mesoamerican ritual to the bars in our pockets.

✦ ✦ ✦

  1. c. 1900 BC

    Origins in Mesoamerica

    The story of chocolate begins with the Theobroma cacao tree, native to the tropical rainforests of Central and South America. Archaeological traces of cacao residue on ancient pottery show that people in the region were preparing cacao drinks more than 3,000 years ago — long before chocolate was ever sweet.

    Early cultures such as the Olmec are believed to be among the first to harvest cacao, roasting and grinding the beans into a bitter, frothy beverage that laid the groundwork for everything that followed.

  2. 250 – 1500 AD

    Sacred Drink of the Maya & Aztec

    For the Maya, cacao was a gift of the gods, woven into rituals, weddings, and daily life. They drank it as a spiced, unsweetened beverage — often flavored with chili, vanilla, or cornmeal and poured between vessels to raise a prized layer of foam.

    The Aztec treasured cacao so highly that its beans were used as currency: a handful could buy food, cloth, or labor. Reserved largely for nobility, priests, and warriors, chocolate was wealth you could drink.

  3. 1500s – 1700s

    Chocolate Crosses to Europe

    When Spanish explorers encountered cacao in the 16th century, they carried it back across the Atlantic. Europeans found the bitter drink strange — until they began adding sugar, honey, and cinnamon, transforming it into a sweet, fashionable luxury.

    Through the 1600s, chocolate houses spread from the Spanish court to France, England, and beyond. Still a drink, and still expensive, it became a symbol of status and sophistication among Europe's elite.

  4. 1800s

    The Industrial Revolution

    The 19th century turned chocolate from a drink into the solid treat we know. In 1828, Coenraad van Houten's cocoa press separated cocoa butter from the bean, producing smooth cocoa powder and making chocolate cheaper and easier to work with.

    Soon after, makers like Fry, Cadbury, Nestlé, and Lindt pushed the craft forward — the first molded eating bars, the invention of milk chocolate (1875), and conching for a melt-in-the-mouth texture. Chocolate became affordable to the many, not just the few.

  5. 1900s – Today

    The Modern Era

    The 20th century brought mass production, global brands, and chocolate in every corner shop. Today the pendulum is swinging back toward quality: bean-to-bar makers celebrate single-origin flavors the way winemakers celebrate terroir.

    Alongside the craft revival comes a reckoning — growing attention to fair trade, farmer livelihoods, and sustainable farming. The next chapter of chocolate's story is about enjoying it more thoughtfully.

Did You Know?

A few sweet morsels of cacao trivia

  • 1

    The word "chocolate" likely derives from the Nahuatl xocolatl.

  • 2

    Aztecs used cacao beans as money — you could buy a turkey for about 100 beans.

  • 3

    It takes roughly a full year's harvest from one cacao tree to make about half a kilo of chocolate.

  • 4

    Milk chocolate wasn't invented until 1875 — chocolate spent most of its history as a drink.
